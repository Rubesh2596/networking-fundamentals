# Practice Questions

## Why this section exists

Practice is how you turn theory into understanding. Reading about networking is useful, but answering questions helps you see whether the ideas are actually making sense.

This section is designed for absolute beginners. It focuses on understanding the basics before moving to more advanced networking topics.

## Beginner practice set

### 1. What is a network?

A network is a group of connected devices that can share information and resources.

### 2. What is the difference between a LAN and a WAN?

A LAN is local and usually small. A WAN covers a larger area and connects different networks together.

### 3. What is the role of a router?

A router connects different networks together and decides where traffic should go.

### 4. What is the role of a switch?

A switch connects devices in the same local network so they can communicate efficiently.

### 5. What is the client-server model?

It is a networking pattern where a client requests a service and a server provides it.

### 6. Why do we use layers in networking?

Layers organize tasks so networking systems become easier to understand, troubleshoot, and build.

## Quick answer key

1. A network is two or more devices connected so they can communicate.
2. A LAN is local; a WAN is broader and often connects multiple LANs.
3. A router connects networks and routes traffic.
4. A switch connects local devices in the same network.
5. The client asks; the server responds.
6. Layers help separate responsibilities and reduce complexity.

## Summary

Practice questions are not just for tests. They are a way to check whether you truly understand the ideas. If you can explain the concept in plain language, you are likely ready to move on to the next lesson.
